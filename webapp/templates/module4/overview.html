Module 4 · Human boundaries in RGB and thermal images

Outline a person with classical OpenCV — no trained detector — then compare that outline with SAM2, which sees the same box. The Fourier page shows how edges and regions come from the frequency domain.

Part 1

RGB photo

GrabCut, watershed, or a fixed-camera background difference. Drag a box around the person and compare with a SAM2 mask.

 Part 2

Thermal image

CLAHE, Otsu, and person-shaped blobs. Warm people are bright; a palette that draws them dark can be inverted.

 Theory demo

Fourier edges

Ideal, Butterworth, and Gaussian high-pass filters, the frequency-domain Laplacian, DoG zero crossings, and Gabor energy.

 Theory

Derivation

Why edges are high frequencies, how the derivative theorem puts the gradient and the Laplacian in the Fourier domain, and a numeric example.

 Report

Printable report

Per-image scores, RGB versus thermal, where classical outlines fail, and the citations.

Code

The segmentation lives in HW3/segment_core.py. SAM2 runs only on your machine (HW3/sam2_masks.py) and saves plain PNG masks the site can read. Step-by-step commands are in HW3/RUN_ASSIGNMENT.md.

  • HW3/segment_rgb.py — one color photo, or every RGB row in prompts.json.
  • HW3/segment_thermal.py — the thermal pipeline.
  • HW3/compare_sam2.py — IoU, Dice, boundary F, Hausdorff, and the summary table.
  • HW3/fourier_segmentation.py — the frequency-domain filters, including the Laplacian check.
