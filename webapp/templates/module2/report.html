Step 1 · Camera calibration

A smartphone camera was calibrated with OpenCV's built-in checkerboard tools (findChessboardCornersSB, cornerSubPix, calibrateCamera) using Zhang's method. The model has focal lengths, principal point and two radial distortion coefficients, with tangential distortion and \(k_3\) fixed at zero (the same model as MATLAB's default).

Step 2 · Real-world 2D dimensions from perspective projection

Pixels are undistorted by inverting \(\mathbf x_d = \mathbf x\,(1 + k_1 r^2 + k_2 r^4)\) with Newton's method. Then one of two models is applied:

  • Homography (reference plane). For points on the object plane \(Z = 0\), \(\lambda\,[u, v, 1]^\top = K[\,r_1\; r_2\; t\,][X, Y, 1]^\top = H[X, Y, 1]^\top\). \(H\) is estimated with the normalized DLT from a checkerboard (54 corners) or a known rectangle (4 corners) on that plane, and \([X, Y, 1]^\top \simeq H^{-1}[u, v, 1]^\top\). Lengths are Euclidean distances between the mapped points.
  • Pinhole at known distance. For a plane perpendicular to the optical axis at taped distance \(Z\), \(X = Z(u - c_x)/f_x,\ Y = Z(v - c_y)/f_y\).

Implementation: HW1/cv_core.py (shared by the command-line scripts and this web application).
