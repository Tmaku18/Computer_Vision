Module 6 · Optical flow, tracking, and structure from motion

Compute how every pixel moves in two short videos, derive Lucas–Kanade and bilinear interpolation and check them on real frame pairs, then reconstruct a flat object from four photographs using the Module 2 camera calibration.

Part 1

Optical flow

Farnebäck dense flow, arrow and color-wheel videos, sparse trails, and what the motion field says about the scene.

 Part 1

Tracking check

Click corners in two frames. The from-scratch tracker, OpenCV, and template matching are scored against your clicks.

 Part 2

Structure from motion

Four views of one flat object. Camera poses, a scaled boundary, and the algebra written out with your numbers.

 Theory

Derivations

Brightness constancy, the aperture problem, Lucas–Kanade, bilinear interpolation, and the SfM chain from E to bundle adjustment.

 Report

Printable report

Flow charts, tracking errors, the four views with camera positions, the workout, and the references.

What you still do by hand

  1. Record two clips, about 30 s each, 1080p at 30 fps. One with the phone still (people or cars moving), one while you walk or pan. Trim them in Photos before uploading.
  2. On the tracking page, click about 10 sharp corners in frame 1, then click where those same corners actually are in frame 2.
  3. Photograph one flat textured object (a book cover, a poster, a box face) from 4 viewpoints, same phone, 1× lens, landscape, so the Module 2 calibration applies. Tape-measure the width and height, and jot down roughly where you stood.
  4. Click the same corners and edge points, in the same order, in all four photos. One length sets the scale; the other checks it.

The same programs run from the command line in HW4/. The step-by-step commands are in HW4/RUN_ASSIGNMENT.md.
