Theory · Flow, tracking, and structure from motion

Derivations used by the flow, tracking, and structure-from-motion pages. References are at the bottom.

1. Brightness constancy

A small patch of surface looks the same in two nearby frames. If a point at \((x, y)\) moves by \((u, v)\) pixels between frame \(t\) and frame \(t+1\),

\[ I(x+u,\, y+v,\, t+1) = I(x, y, t). \]

Taylor-expand the left side around \((x, y, t)\) and drop the higher-order terms:

\[ I + I_x u + I_y v + I_t \approx I \quad\Rightarrow\quad I_x u + I_y v + I_t = 0. \]

\(I_x\) and \(I_y\) are spatial derivatives, \(I_t\) is the frame difference, and \((u, v)\) is the unknown motion. This is one equation in two unknowns. Any \((u, v)\) that differs by a slide along the image edge still satisfies it. That is the aperture problem: a window that only sees an edge cannot tell motion along the edge from no motion at all.

2. Lucas–Kanade

Lucas and Kanade assume the motion is constant inside a small window and stack one brightness equation per pixel. With \(A\) the matrix whose rows are \((I_x, I_y)\) and \(b = -I_t\),

\[ A \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \end{bmatrix} = b, \qquad (A^\top A)\begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \end{bmatrix} = A^\top b. \]

The \(2\times 2\) matrix \(A^\top A\) is the structure tensor of the window:

\[ A^\top A = \sum \begin{bmatrix} I_x^2 & I_x I_y \\ I_x I_y & I_y^2 \end{bmatrix}. \]

Its eigenvalues say whether the window can be tracked. Two large eigenvalues: a corner, and \((u, v)\) is determined. One large and one tiny: an edge, and only the component across the edge is determined. Both tiny: a flat patch, and the equations have no information.

The linearization is only good for a fraction of a pixel, so the step is repeated. That repetition is Gauss–Newton on the sum of squared brightness differences. A coarse-to-fine pyramid (Bouguet) lets the same small-motion solver handle displacements of several pixels: solve on a downsampled pair, double the displacement, and refine on the next finer level.

3. The same idea as minimizing SSD

For two frames the quantity being minimized is the sum of squared differences

\[ E(u, v) = \sum_{x, y \in W} \big(I_2(x+u, y+v) - I_1(x, y)\big)^2. \]

Differentiating and setting the derivatives to zero, then replacing the derivatives of \(I_2\) by the derivatives of the template, gives the same normal equations. Each Gauss–Newton step solves them and adds \((\Delta u, \Delta v)\) to the running displacement. The tracking page prints one such step on a \(5\times 5\) window: the intensities, \(I_x\), \(I_y\), \(A^\top A\), \(A^\top b\), and the solution.

4. Bilinear interpolation

The displacement is not an integer, so \(I_2(x+u, y+v)\) has to be read between pixels. Let \(x_0 = \lfloor x \rfloor\), \(a = x - x_0\), and likewise \(y_0\) and \(b\). Interpolate in \(x\) on each of the two rows, then in \(y\) between those results:

\[ \begin{aligned} I(x, y_0) &= (1-a)\,I_{00} + a\,I_{10}, \\ I(x, y_0+1) &= (1-a)\,I_{01} + a\,I_{11}, \\ I(x, y) &= (1-b)\,I(x, y_0) + b\,I(x, y_0+1). \end{aligned} \]

Collecting terms,

\[ I(x, y) = (1-a)(1-b)\,I_{00} + a(1-b)\,I_{10} + (1-a)b\,I_{01} + ab\,I_{11}. \]

On an image that is itself a linear ramp, \(I = c_0 + c_1 x + c_2 y\), this formula is exact. The unit test checks that identity.

5. Structure from motion

A 3D point \(X\) seen by two calibrated cameras projects to pixels \(x\) and \(x'\). With the first camera at the origin, the second is a rotation \(R\) and a translation \(t\), and the two rays and the baseline are coplanar:

\[ \tilde{x}'^{\top} E \tilde{x} = 0, \qquad E = [t]_{\times} R. \]

\(E\) is the essential matrix. Its SVD \(E = U \operatorname{diag}(1, 1, 0) V^{\top}\) produces four candidates \((R, t)\), from the two ways to build a rotation with the matrix \(W\) and from the sign of \(t = U_{:3}\). Triangulating the points and keeping the candidate that puts them in front of both cameras (positive depth, cheirality) selects one.

A point observed only on a plane does not determine \(E\) uniquely: many essential matrices share the same correspondences. The object in this assignment is flat, so the two-view pose is taken from the homography instead, \(H = K (R + t n^{\top}/d) K^{-1}\), which decomposes into four poses of its own. Cheirality again picks one. The workout still forms \(E = [t]_{\times} R\) for that pose and prints the SVD, so the essential-matrix algebra is visible with the numbers that the reconstruction actually used. findEssentialMat is run as well; its inlier count is reported next to the homography pose.

Linear triangulation writes the cross product \(\tilde{x} \times P X = 0\) as two rows per camera and solves the resulting \(4\times 4\) system by SVD. That is the DLT block in the workout. Views 3 and 4 are then attached by PnP: the already triangulated points are 3D–2D correspondences, and solvePnP returns each new \((R, t)\).

Bundle adjustment treats every camera (except the first, which stays at the origin) and every 3D point as unknowns and minimizes the sum of squared reprojection errors with Levenberg–Marquardt. Reprojection is unchanged if all points and all translations are multiplied by the same constant, so the reconstruction has no absolute size. One tape-measured edge supplies that scale. The boundary points are then projected onto their best-fit plane (the smallest singular vector of the centered points) and connected into a polygon whose edge lengths and area can be compared with the tape.

Camera centers in the first camera’s frame are \(C = -R^{\top} t\), in meters after scaling.
