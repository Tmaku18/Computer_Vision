Theory · calibrated stereo, and what to write by hand

Part 1 may be typed. Part 2 may not: the uncalibrated derivation has to be handwritten and scanned. Camera photos of the paper are not accepted. The outline and the numbers below are the raw material, not the derivation.

1. Calibrated stereo

The same phone is used at two stops a distance \(B\) apart, with no rotation. A scene point at depth \(Z\) projects to \(u\) in the left photo and to \(u - d\) in the right photo. Similar triangles give

\[ \frac{d}{f} = \frac{B}{Z} \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad Z = \frac{f B}{d}. \]

The pinhole equations then place the point in the left camera's coordinates:

\[ X = \frac{(u - c_x) Z}{f}, \qquad Y = \frac{(v - c_y) Z}{f}. \]

The size of an edge is the distance between its two endpoints. A rectangle reports the average of each pair of opposite edges, so width and height. A circle is fit in the plane of the rim points (the plane normal is the smallest singular vector of the centered points); the diameter is twice that radius. Any other polygon reports every edge.

Differentiating the depth formula shows where the measurement is fragile:

\[ \left|\delta Z\right| \approx \frac{Z^{2}}{f B}\,\delta d. \]

A one-pixel mismatch hurts much more at a larger depth, and a longer slide (larger \(B\)) reduces it. Stereo calibration, with the Module 2 intrinsics held fixed, estimates the real rotation and translation between the stops. \(|T|\) should match the ruler. Rectification then puts matching points on the same row, which is why the search is one-dimensional.

Zhang, 2000, for the intrinsics. Hartley and Zisserman, chapters 9–12, for the two-view geometry. Hirschmüller, 2008, for the semiglobal disparity map used on the room page.

2. Uncalibrated stereo — write this by hand

Do not submit this list as the derivation. Write the algebra yourself, using the numbers from the button below, and scan it.

  1. The epipolar constraint \(x'^{\top} F x = 0\). Eight point pairs determine \(F\), after Hartley normalization and the rank-2 fix.
  2. A canonical projective reconstruction: \(P = [I \mid 0]\) and \(P' = [[e']_{\times} F \mid e']\).
  3. Why that reconstruction does not preserve lengths, so the object size is not determined yet.
  4. The metric upgrade: estimate one focal length (Bougnoux, square pixels, principal point at the image center), form \(E = K^{\top} F K\), factor \(E\) into \(R\) and \(t\), and set \(\|t\| = B\) or to one known length.
  5. Triangulate the clicked points and measure, the same way as in part 1.

A pure sideways slide puts the epipoles at infinity. The focal length then drops out of \(F\), and Bougnoux's formula has nothing to return. In that case the handwritten step should say so and use the Module 2 focal length.
